space. You land in front of a black hole, and as you scroll the camera drifts towards it before
the page opens up into everything else.

## How it's made

The black hole isn't a video or a picture. It's drawn live in your browser with WebGL2: a small
program that works out, pixel by pixel, how light bends around it, with a glowing disk of gas
spinning past. The same idea paints the soft nebula skies behind the cards.

The movement comes from GSAP. Headings rise out of their masks, sections glide into place, the
projects slide sideways as you scroll, and each scroll step settles on a full screen instead of
stopping halfway between two. Lenis keeps the scrolling itself smooth.

I cared about the small things too. The loading screen counts real progress, not a fake timer.
The email buttons let you pick Gmail, Outlook or your phone's own mail app, with the subject
already written. Every section fits on one screen, from small laptops to wide monitors, the site
works just as well on a phone, and it calms down if your device asks for reduced motion.

It's built to be quick and easy to find as well. The heavy 3D work waits until the page needs it,
search engines and AI assistants get a readable copy of every section, and every change passes
automatic checks before it goes live.
